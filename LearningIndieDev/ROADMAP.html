Cellular Automata Roguelike Roadmap v2.2

Status: Active production roadmap | Version: 2.2 | Updated: 2026-09-17 | Cadence: Two weeks, approximately 20 hours per developer | Product owner: Josh

This is the product-level source of truth for what the team is trying to make, what comes next, and what must be true before work moves forward. Feature briefs, sprint control records, Trello cards, and technical plans provide execution detail; they do not silently expand this roadmap.

Product outcome

Deliver a run-based game built around cellular automata as a roguelike. The player guides one species through six ten-second simulation rounds in one continuing ecosystem. The player makes five temporary Mutation decisions per run; permanent currency purchases in Gene Lab add Genome Upgrades to a species' Genome skill tree. The long-term objective is a diverse, resilient ecosystem rather than one dominant species.

The first vertical slice uses:

  • Scenario: Forest Edge.
  • Player species: Hare.
  • Opposing species: Fox. Playable plant species are on hold and are not part of the current expedition contract.
  • Builds: Trailblazer, Warren, and Gardeners.
  • Home base: the GalapagOS Desktop. Lab features such as Gene Lab, Species Collection, History, and Expedition Planning live within that player-facing home. The standalone Lab scene remains a legacy/developer route until its retained tests and responsibilities are deliberately migrated.

Decisions that are already locked

  • One expedition contains six ten-second simulation rounds in the same evolving world, for one minute of simulation time.
  • Round 1 has no upgrade. After rounds 1–5, the player chooses one of three Mutations or Skip. Finishing round 6 goes to results without another choice.
  • Mutations are temporary for the current run and exclusive to Species Simulations. Each is communicated with a small icon and keyword, not a full statistic breakdown; its expected impact should be clear in the following round.
  • Pause is available. There is no Restart action. End abandons the run after a Yes confirmation and forfeits rewards if used before round 6.
  • Victory means the player species survives through round 6. Extinction ends the run immediately as a failure with no rewards.
  • Successful-run currency uses a performance measure now in development, not simply final population. A Skip bonus and additional bonus-event rewards are undecided and non-blocking; if a Skip bonus is adopted, it uses Genome Upgrade currency.
  • Permanent Genome Upgrades are bought with currency in the Gene Lab application on the GalapagOS Desktop and fill a Genome skill tree.
  • Forest Edge board size is deferred; playable plant species, including Fern, are on hold and are not contract requirements.
  • Genome nodes remain unlocked permanently, while each species has a separate active Genome configuration that can change only between simulations.
  • Every participating species receives its frozen active Genome at launch, including species that are not controlled by the player.
  • The first Genome identity, local-profile, immutable snapshot, authoring-type, catalog, and Gene Lab display contracts are implemented. No production node effect, cost, purchase, activation action, migration, or approved map is implied by that foundation.
  • The current GalapagOS Desktop acceptance route directly starts Forest Edge/Hare. The cause of the recorded PlayMode failures has not been verified from individual test results; preserving the current route does not waive the required profile and launch-request handoff.
  • Species and Biome Simulations use separate success scorecards. A result from one mode is not sufficient approval evidence for the other.
  • Active-run disk save/resume is separate from profile, settings, and settled progression saves.
  • Upgrade, species, scenario, and balance work follows TOK0END.
  • Research findings remain bounded to their declared scenario, values, seeds, windows, and review decision. EX-010 does not authorize a broader predictive or balance claim.

Explicit holds and non-goals

The following work is not part of the initial vertical slice unless a sprint review deliberately promotes a smaller slice:

  • broad species, biome, scenario, or Genome-tree production;
  • cloud sync, multiple profiles, or active-expedition disk resume;
  • a generalized modifier, scripting, rule-plugin, event-bus, or navigation framework;
  • full reactive ecology, hidden rubber-banding, or a large random-event catalog;
  • predictive-model promotion or a new research program without a frozen question and human review capacity;
  • final-volume art or audio production before comprehension evidence approves the direction;
  • colony construction, ant tunnels, beaver dams, or other geometry-directed mechanics outside a separately approved experiment.

Roadmap review rule

  1. The playable outcome demonstrated and its evidence.
  2. Planned versus actual effort by owner.
  3. Acceptance failures, defects, and changed product assumptions.
  4. Work completed, cut, carried, or returned to the horizon.
  5. The next sprint's single primary outcome, task-level estimates, reviewer, and exit test.

Update this roadmap only when milestone scope, dependency order, effort band, landing window, or product direction materially changes. Implementation status belongs in the sprint control record and task board.
