# Interpretation: Trailblazer early-choice timing

The tick-0 choice is not a useful balance fix based on this evidence. It changed the strict all-species clear rate from 15% to 14%, and the matched interval is wide enough to include a modest benefit or harm. Both rates remain far below the exploratory 40–50% goal, so the timing shift does not move the strategy toward the target in a reliable way.

There is a small, mixed opening-phase signal. Hares ended phase one at a median of 26 instead of 21, and median Hare combat deaths were 1.5 lower. Total Hare deaths did not improve, starvation deaths were slightly higher, and the readable slashline metrics moved only modestly. Foxes also ended phase one at a lower median population, with slightly fewer births and food consumed and more energy-blocked reproduction candidates. The data do not establish that the early skill caused those small median changes to compound into the lower number of runs reaching tick 600; the paired clear test itself is inconclusive.

The next balancing step should stay focused on the limiting ecology already visible in these runs: Fox food and energy access, starvation, and reproduction. Inspect those measures by phase among all runs, including runs that end early, before changing Fox values. Then choose one specific, reversible Fox-side intervention to test on common seeds against the unchanged Trailblazer and no-choice controls. Keep the 40–50% node target and the minimum-five Hare/Fox finish rule fixed during that comparison.

Do not adopt an earlier player choice as a production change from this test. No player-facing timing, skill values, or authored scenario defaults were modified.

## October 7 failure review

A read-only follow-up classified every matched run ending before tick 600 as Hare extinction: 79/200 with current timing and 98/200 with early timing. The early arm therefore has 19 more early Hare-extinction runs, despite its higher opening-phase median Hare population. This secondary observation is exploratory; it does not establish the causal mechanism or overturn the inconclusive paired clear test.

The Fox phase medians also show a tradeoff worth diagnosing. In phase 2, current versus early timing gives 24.5 versus 30 prey kills and 15 versus 11.5 starvation deaths. In phase 3, the corresponding medians are 18 versus 28 kills and 10 versus 6 starvation deaths. These observed windows include partial terminal phases. Better Fox feeding and persistence can coexist with worse Hare outcomes, so a general Fox energy buff is not yet justified.

The next experiment should first separate early Hare extinction from horizon-reaching failures that miss the Plant, Hare, or Fox threshold. Compare phase-specific Hare combat/starvation and Fox feeding/starvation using the same seeds and exposure denominators. Fox mating-energy eligibility remains one possible counterfactual, not an approved change. Current authored Fox eligibility is 25% of 240 maximum energy (60); each parent's mating energy cost is 15% (36). A killed Hare supplies its authored energy value of 96. Blocked-candidate counts do not record the exact energy at every event, so they cannot prove which threshold is responsible.
