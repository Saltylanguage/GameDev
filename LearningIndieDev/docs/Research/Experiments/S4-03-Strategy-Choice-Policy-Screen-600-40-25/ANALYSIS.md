# Interpretation

Gardeners is the only tested build with a clear improvement over skipping every upgrade: it cleared 51/200 nodes (25.5%) versus 32/200 (16.0%), a matched gain of 9.5 percentage points (95% CI 3.2 to 15.8; exact McNemar p=0.0054). That is a useful follow-up signal, but it remains below the agreed rough 40–50% target. Trailblazer (15.0%) and Warren (17.0%) did not separate from control in this screen. These three comparisons were exploratory and their p-values are not adjusted for multiple comparisons.

Among the runs that reached tick 600, Gardeners had median Plants/Hares above the clear thresholds but median Foxes of 3. Fox populations fell below five at the tick-600 boundary in the median run for every arm. Across runs, median Fox starvation deaths were 47–48 and median candidates blocked by energy were roughly 6,700–7,700, compared with median births of 40–45. This points to Fox persistence, food access, and reproduction energy as the next diagnosis. It does not establish a single cause or justify changing attack accuracy or any balance value yet.

The player-readable Hare slashlines tell a consistent supporting story: Gardeners had higher median eAVI and sAVI, with less negative RFS and APS, while pAVI stayed close to control. These aggregate measures help describe the run, but the explicit all-species clear rule remains the outcome measure. The strategy policy was non-adaptive and could choose only from the real generated offer; target skills appeared on 76–81% of recorded decisions. Skips when neither target skill was offered are part of this policy test, not evidence about an adaptive player.

Phase activity adds a second Hare pressure signal. Before the first choice (phase 1, ticks 0–100), every arm had the same median fall from 40 to 21 Hares, with 45 Hare deaths: 39 combat and 6 starvation. Since the first mutation choice happens after tick 100, none of the tested builds could affect this opening loss. Hare populations then generally rebounded during the middle phases. In phase 6, median Hare starvation deaths rose to 37–60 per run, while Fox populations were already low; median Fox deaths and births were near zero in this final window. Phase-6 rows include runs that ended early within that phase, so these are window medians, not all tick-600 populations. This pattern suggests an opening predator-pressure problem followed by late food/energy pressure, rather than one continuous Hare problem.

This evidence covers one wrapped 600-tick Forest Edge node and one fixed initial population. It does not show journey-level success, a balanced long-term ecology, or human enjoyment. The next useful comparison is a timing-only Trailblazer counterfactual: shift the same five picks one phase earlier (ticks 0, 100, 200, 300, 400 instead of 100–500) on the same seeds. That tests whether early predator deaths are reachable through player choice while holding the upgrade path, pick count, populations, and simulation values fixed. Track whether Hare combat deaths fall without worsening Fox survival or the all-species clear rate. Treat this as a diagnostic of choice timing, not a recommended UX change. Do not tune from the present medians alone.
